EMI is the fixed amount you pay every month to repay a loan. It covers part of the loan and part of the interest. This calculator uses the standard reducing-balance formula that most banks use.

Try different amounts, rates and times. A longer time lowers the EMI but increases the total interest you pay.

How to use

  1. Type the loan amount in rupees.
  2. Type the yearly interest rate and the time, in years or months.
  3. Read the EMI, the total interest and the total payment below.

Tips

  • Compare a few times (for example 5, 7 and 10 years) to see how much interest you save with a shorter loan.
  • Add processing fees and insurance separately, as they are not in the EMI.
  • If your rate changes (floating rate), come back and calculate again with the new rate.

Questions

How is EMI calculated?

EMI = P x r x (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n - 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly rate (yearly rate divided by 12 and by 100) and n is the number of months.

Does it work for home, car and personal loans?

Yes. The formula is the same. Only the amount, rate and time are different.

Why does my bank show a slightly different EMI?

Banks may round, add fees, or have a different start date. Treat this as an estimate and check your loan offer.

Do you store my numbers?

No. The calculation happens in your browser.
